How Condorcet works

Three steps from your ballot to the voting record.

  1. 1

    Find your race

    Search by state, or pick from the list of 2026 Senate races. Each race shows the two candidates on the general-election ballot.

  2. 2

    Pick up to five issues

    Choose from the 32 policy areas Congress's own researchers use to label every bill, so an issue means the same thing for both candidates.

  3. 3

    Compare the votes

    For each issue, see up to five recent bills side by side: a short summary of what each bill does and how each candidate voted.

Where the data comes from

Votes and bill records come from Congress.gov and Senate.gov. Each summary is shortened from the official Congressional Research Service summary, and every bill links to its source.

What the colors mean

Voted yeaVoted for the bill.
Voted nayVoted against the bill.
Not votingWas absent or didn't vote.

A vote for a bill isn't a stance on the issue. Read the summary to see what the bill does.

What we don't do

We don't score candidates, recommend anyone, or guess where they stand. If a candidate has never served in Congress, we say so and link to their campaign site instead of leaving a blank column.
